# Wafier — Verified Primary-Source Legal and Regulatory Research

## Research Scope and Bottom-Line Determinations

This review was conducted against publicly accessible primary or first-party sources from Saudi regulators and public bodies—principally SDAIA’s National Data Governance Platform, the Saudi Central Bank (SAMA), the Capital Market Authority (CMA), the Saudi Electricity Regulatory Authority (SERA), Saudi Energy’s official site, Umm Al-Qura, KAPSARC, and the ITU. Where an underlying PDF or detailed framework could not itself be retrieved and checked, it is not treated as an enforcement-grade source.

The most important conclusions for the Wafier submission are these:

**PDPL does cover identifiable household electricity-consumption data, but the claim “explicit consent is always legally required for household kWh data” would be too strong.** The PDPL defines personal data broadly enough to capture meter/consumption information when it can identify a person directly or indirectly, while its statutory list of “Sensitive Data” does not specifically include electricity consumption. The PDPL establishes consent as a primary basis subject to statutory exceptions, while the Implementing Regulation reserves *explicit* consent for Sensitive Data, Credit Data, and decisions made solely on automated processing. citeturn21view3turn20view2 Wafier may—and for a hackathon demonstrator probably should—use an affirmative, explicit opt-in as a product-level privacy control, but it should not describe that as an unconditional statutory requirement merely because the input is household electricity usage.

**The PDPL does not categorically prohibit targeted advertising using all personal data.** Instead, purpose limitation prevents silent repurposing, a separate consent is required for each processing purpose, and direct marketing has its own consent and opt-out requirements. The Law expressly allows non-sensitive personal data to be processed for marketing where it was collected directly and consent was given. citeturn19view1turn19view3turn20view2turn20view4 Accordingly, Wafier can implement a stronger deterministic rule—“household consumption data is never used for advertising”—but should label that a **Wafier privacy policy control**, rather than falsely presenting it as a blanket statutory Saudi advertising ban.

**Anonymization and pseudonymization are legally different.** SDAIA's Regulation requires anonymization to make re-identification impossible; once that standard is achieved, the information is no longer personal data. Pseudonymization merely replaces principal identifiers with codes and retains separately held information capable of reconnecting the data to the subject, so pseudonymized data remains within the data-protection risk perimeter. citeturn20view2turn21view0 There is no general PDPL sentence saying “all analytics exports must first be anonymized.” For scientific/research/statistical processing without consent, however, the Law and Regulation impose specific de-identification/pseudonymization safeguards. citeturn19view3turn20view4

**SDAIA's AI Ethics Principles strongly support transparency, explanation, human-controllable AI, model monitoring and anti-deception, but they do not contain an express prohibition worded as “AI financial forecasts must not be guaranteed.”** Principle 7 instead states that AI systems should never lead people to be deceived, while Principles 5–7 require reliability testing, explainability, accountable ownership and human oversight. citeturn26view0turn26view1turn26view2 Thus, a Wafier policy-node rule prohibiting “guaranteed bill” language is a well-grounded implementation inference, not a quoted Saudi AI rule.

**SAMA Open Banking is about financial-institution/payment-account data, not raw electricity-meter telemetry.** SAMA describes Open Banking as customers of financial institutions securely sharing their financial data with third-party providers; the Payments Implementing Regulations define Payment Account Information Service around payment accounts and associated transactions. citeturn25view0turn25view1turn25view2 A utility payment visible in a bank account may fall within account-information data, whereas meter kWh, interval load and smart-meter telemetry do not become Open Banking data merely because Wafier is entered in a “Finance” hackathon track.

**CMA's FinTech Lab is oriented to capital-market activity.** CMA says the laboratory operates under its capital-market supervisory role, and its earlier ExPermit announcement expressly referred to applications involving securities businesses under CMA supervision. A utility-budget forecasting application with no investment advice, securities execution, arranging, crowdfunding, portfolio management or similar capital-market function therefore appears outside that boundary on the stated facts. citeturn25view4turn25view5turn25view6

**SERA's current public residential tariff page states two residential bands in its `HH/kWh` tariff column: 1–6,000 kWh/month = 18; more than 6,000 kWh/month = 30.** The official SERA result was retrieved in this research on August 30, 2026 and shows the page indexed March 18, 2026. citeturn22search0 The tool could not directly render that SERA HTML page, so Wafier should preserve the tariff provenance and effective-date/version in its deterministic tariff table rather than hard-code the values without source/version metadata.

**I did not verify a public Saudi Energy/SEC statement granting consumer-selected third-party apps direct access to smart-meter interval data or documenting a public smart-meter API.** Saudi Energy publishes a general Data Sharing page and a privacy notice allowing forms of third-party sharing, but those materials do not establish an OAuth-like consumer delegation right or third-party smart-meter API. citeturn22search1turn24search1turn24search4 This is one of the clearest infrastructure/policy gaps affecting the Wafier architecture.

**No Saudi primary source reviewed establishes a bespoke cause of action or liability allocation specifically for a wrong AI household-bill forecast.** Saudi Arabia instead has general civil liability: Article 120 of the Civil Transactions Law provides that a fault causing damage to another entails compensation, while Articles 136–137 address full compensation and loss/lost gain resulting naturally from the harmful act. citeturn29view0turn29view2 PDPL Article 40 separately permits compensation for material or moral damage from PDPL violations. citeturn19view4 The absence of an AI-forecast-specific allocation rule should therefore be presented as a **policy gap**, not as immunity from liability.

### Source hierarchy for enforcement logic

For Wafier's deterministic policy node, the hierarchy should be **binding Saudi law/regulation first**, then regulator rules applicable to the actual regulated activity, followed by official non-binding ethics/guidance, and finally ITU/KAPSARC material for architecture and advice grounding. SDAIA itself explicitly warns that its PDPL Guide is explanatory and not a binding legal instrument. citeturn18search2

The records safest to encode as legal policy rules are therefore VR-PDPL-01 through VR-PDPL-09 and applicable SAMA/CMA rules **only if Wafier's actual functionality places it within those regulators' perimeter**. SDAIA AI Ethics, KAPSARC and Y.3172 should be represented as governance/architecture/reference controls rather than mislabelled as statutes. citeturn25view3turn25view6turn26view3

## Findings by Research Question

**PDPL — consent for household electricity data.** Identifiable electricity-consumption history falls within the broad definition of Personal Data when the meter/account/household information can identify an individual directly or indirectly. Electricity usage is not named in PDPL's Sensitive Data list. citeturn21view3turn30view0 Article 5 says that, except for cases provided by the Law, personal data may not be processed or its processing purpose changed without consent, while Article 6 provides alternative grounds including implementation of a prior agreement, statutory processing and qualifying legitimate interests. citeturn20view0

The critical drafting point is that **“consent” and “explicit consent” are not interchangeable statutory claims**. Implementing Regulation Article 11 requires consent to be free, non-misleading, purpose-specific, explained, documented and separate for each processing purpose; paragraph 2 specifically requires explicit consent for Sensitive Data, Credit Data and decisions made solely on automated processing. citeturn20view2 A Wafier bill estimate displayed to a user is not necessarily a legal “decision” merely because it is produced automatically. Accordingly, the safest hackathon language is:

> **Wafier design control:** affirmative, documented consent is obtained before linking or ingesting identifiable household electricity data, even where another legal basis might potentially be available. This is a privacy-by-design choice; the app does not assert that household kWh data is inherently Sensitive Data under PDPL.

That distinction is directly supported by the statutory definitions and consent rules. citeturn21view3turn20view2

**PDPL — purpose limitation and advertising.** The Law permits processing for the purpose for which data was collected and lists exceptions for a different purpose. It also requires the collection notice to tell the individual that subsequent processing will not be inconsistent with the stated purpose outside the permitted Article 10 cases. citeturn19view1 The Regulation strengthens this operationally by requiring separate consent for each processing purpose. citeturn20view2

There is therefore a strong legal basis for a deterministic gate such as:

`purpose = electricity_forecast` → allowed under the recorded basis;  
`purpose = targeted_marketing` → **deny unless a separate legally valid marketing basis/consent exists**.

But a claim that “PDPL bans targeted advertising” would be inaccurate. PDPL Article 26 affirmatively contemplates marketing of non-sensitive personal data where it was collected directly and consent was obtained, and Regulation Article 29 requires consent plus an easy opt-out for direct marketing. citeturn19view3turn20view4 A categorical Wafier rule never to monetize or use meter consumption for ad targeting would therefore be **stronger than the statutory minimum**.

**PDPL — anonymization, pseudonymization and analytics export.** Implementing Regulation Article 9 sets a high bar for anonymization: re-identification must be impossible, risks must be evaluated, techniques must be updated, and effectiveness must be reviewed; truly anonymized information then ceases to be Personal Data. citeturn20view2 Pseudonymization has a deliberately weaker definition: identifying fields are converted to codes and the additional information needed to relink them is kept separately. citeturn21view0 Consequently, an exported dataset identified only by `household_hash` should **not automatically be labelled “anonymous.”**

For scientific, research or statistical processing without consent, PDPL Article 27 provides specific paths involving non-identifiability or destruction of identity evidence before disclosure, and Regulation Article 30 requires pseudonymization when compatible with the purpose. citeturn19view3turn20view4 These provisions do **not** establish a universal rule that every commercial analytics transfer or every export must be anonymized first. A Wafier rule requiring anonymization/pseudonymization before analytics export is nevertheless defensible as privacy-by-design; its UI/legal documentation should distinguish that internal safeguard from the narrower statutory requirements.

**PDPL — rights and remedies.** Article 4 gives individuals the rights to know the legal basis/purpose, access their data, receive it in readable form, correct/update it and request destruction subject to the Law's exceptions. citeturn20view0 The Regulation also requires destruction where data is no longer necessary, consent is withdrawn and is the sole basis, or processing is unlawful. citeturn20view3 Article 34 permits a complaint to the competent authority; Article 36 provides for administrative warnings/fines for violations outside Article 35's criminal case; and Article 40 allows a damaged individual to seek material or moral compensation in a competent court. citeturn20view1turn19view4

For Wafier, that supports an account-level **Privacy Center** permitting access/export, correction, consent withdrawal and deletion requests, with a complaint/contact pathway. The product should not promise unconditional deletion where a separate statutory retention requirement applies. citeturn20view3

**SDAIA AI Ethics — transparency and explainability.** Principle 6 requires AI systems to have clarity and explainability, trace automated decision-making stages, communicate relevant system purpose/processes, and provide understandable explanations to affected stakeholders. It also calls for model-decision information in the interface, traceable datasets/processes and an opportunity to request explanatory information. citeturn26view0turn26view1 For Wafier, mandatory RAG citations are an especially strong implementation of traceability, although the SDAIA document does not itself say every generative answer must have a citation.

A defensible forecast explanation could therefore expose the decisive elements—recent kWh history, weather input window, tariff version, forecast horizon, confidence/uncertainty and whether the forecast was adjusted by a deterministic policy rule. This is an implementation inference from SDAIA's explainability and traceability requirements. citeturn26view0turn26view1

**SDAIA AI Ethics — human oversight.** Principle 5 says automated decisions with irreversible or difficult-to-reverse effects should trigger human oversight and final determination; Principle 7 more generally calls for human-controlled AI whose functions can be monitored and intervened in by authorized users. citeturn26view0turn26view2 Wafier is comparatively low consequence if it merely displays estimates and suggestions rather than automatically cutting power, moving funds, executing investments or changing essential services. Human escalation remains appropriate when the model is out-of-distribution, the tariff source is stale, a user disputes the estimate, or the system proposes a consequential financial action.

**SDAIA AI Ethics — “guaranteed” predictions.** There is **no verified sentence in the SDAIA AI Ethics Principles expressly prohibiting “financial-like guarantees from AI predictions.”** What is explicit is that AI systems should never lead people to be deceived, that inaccurate-prediction risks should be mitigated, that model performance should be monitored, and that accountability must be attributable. citeturn26view1turn26view2 Therefore the hackathon documentation can accurately say:

> **Derived policy control, not a verbatim regulation:** Wafier must never present predicted SAR expenditure as guaranteed, exact, regulator-approved or certain. Forecasts are labelled estimates and disclose material uncertainty.

For entities actually subject to SAMA's Financial Consumer Protection Principles, this approach is reinforced by requirements that consumer information be accurate and not misleading and that misinformation, fraud and deception be avoided. citeturn25view3 Those SAMA provisions should not, however, be represented as automatically binding on an unregulated energy-budget app.

**SAMA Open Banking — boundary.** The public SAMA definition repeatedly anchors Open Banking to customers of financial institutions sharing financial data. citeturn25view0turn25view1 The Payments Implementing Regulations make the boundary considerably more concrete: Payment Account Information Service concerns consolidated information from Payment Accounts, and an AISP may access only designated Payment Accounts and associated Payment Transactions. citeturn25view2

The resulting data classification for Wafier is:

| Data item | SAMA Open Banking characterization |
|---|---|
| Smart-meter 15/30/60-minute kWh readings | **Not demonstrated to be Open Banking data** by the reviewed SAMA sources. |
| Utility meter identifier / premise consumption history | **Utility data, not payment-account data** on the reviewed sources. |
| Electricity bill debit visible in user's bank account | Potentially part of the relevant **Payment Account / Payment Transaction** information if accessed through an AIS arrangement. |
| Bank balance used by a budgeting feature | Financial/payment-account information; SAMA perimeter becomes materially relevant. |
| Weather temperature/humidity | Not Open Banking data. |

This distinction follows from SAMA's own definitions. citeturn25view0turn25view2

A significant verification limitation is that SAMA's current public Open Banking site says users seeking access to the Open Banking Framework and Lab should contact the Open Banking Team; I therefore did **not** use an unofficially mirrored copy of the detailed Framework as an enforcement source. citeturn25view0 Scope conclusions above are supported independently by SAMA's public announcement and the binding Payments Implementing Regulations. citeturn25view1turn25view2

**SAMA consumer-protection rules — forecast disclaimers.** SAMA's Principles require a financial institution to give clear, comprehensible, accurate and non-misleading product information and require warning statements concerning potential risks; Rule 2 expressly directs financial institutions to avoid misinformation, fraud and deception. citeturn25view3 This is highly useful drafting precedent for Wafier's forecast language:

> “Estimated electricity spend based on available consumption, weather and tariff inputs. Actual bills may differ because of usage changes, meter/billing adjustments, weather error, tariff changes, taxes or other charges.”

The exact Wafier disclaimer above is a proposed implementation, not SAMA-prescribed text. More importantly, the SAMA source expressly says these Principles form the framework for consumers of **financial institutions**. citeturn25view3 Unless Wafier performs a regulated financial/payment activity or operates through a supervised institution, this should be cited as an **analogous consumer-protection benchmark**, not falsely asserted as a direct Wafier obligation.

**CMA FinTech Lab — boundary.** CMA's own mandate concerns the capital market, securities trading and investor protection. citeturn25view6 Its FinTech Lab is an experimental environment under CMA supervision, while an official ExPermit announcement expressly characterized relevant applications as those involving securities businesses falling under CMA supervision. citeturn25view4turn25view5 On the project description provided—forecasting electricity spend and helping a household stay within a monthly budget—Wafier does not appear to be carrying out a securities business merely because the hackathon category is labelled “Finance.”

That conclusion must be revisited if the product later introduces investment recommendations, automated investing, securities crowdfunding, arranging securities transactions, portfolio management, or other capital-market functionality; regulator jurisdiction is based on the activity, not the hackathon track label. This is an inference from CMA's published mandate and FinTech Lab scope. citeturn25view4turn25view6

**Electricity tariffs.** SERA's official Consumption Tariff result currently shows the residential categories as `1–6000` kWh/month with `18` in its `HH/KWh` tariff column, and `More than 6000` with `30`. citeturn22search0 Because tariff configuration is a deterministic legal/economic input rather than an ML prediction, it belongs naturally in Wafier's Y.3172 **P (policy)** stage rather than being learned by the model. ITU defines P as the node in which policies are applied to the model output. citeturn28view3

A production-quality implementation should store at least `authority`, `source_url`, `retrieved_at`, `effective_from` where known, `tier_threshold`, `rate_unit`, and a hash/version of the tariff source. That avoids letting a language model invent or silently update tariff logic.

**Smart-meter sharing with third-party apps.** Saudi Energy publicly maintains a Data Sharing framework and a privacy policy allowing certain third-party disclosure. citeturn22search1turn24search1 It also provides a meter-declaration process recognizing relationships such as owner and tenant. citeturn24search0 I nevertheless found **no verified public Saudi Energy/SERA statement in this source set saying that an electricity customer can authorize an independent app such as Wafier to obtain smart-meter interval readings through a public API**.

That distinction matters. “Saudi Energy may share data with service providers” and “Saudi Energy has a general data-sharing request policy” do not establish an end-user OAuth/delegation scheme. citeturn24search1turn24search4 Wafier's hackathon architecture should consequently model smart-meter ingestion as an **integration dependency / policy gap**, with a permitted demo fallback such as user-provided bills or consumption data, rather than claiming an official production SEC API that has not been verified.

**Energy-efficiency grounding for the RAG assistant.** KAPSARC's official cooling publication identifies thermostat choice, time the AC operates, number/area of rooms cooled, AC efficiency, dwelling characteristics and user behavior as major determinants of residential cooling electricity demand, and supports greater adoption of efficient cooling technologies. citeturn28view4 These are suitable citation-backed RAG themes.

The safest grounded advice is therefore qualitative: reduce unnecessary cooling duration/space, use efficient cooling equipment, manage thermostat settings sensibly, and recognize that building characteristics and behavior materially affect consumption. citeturn28view4 This research did **not** verify an official source in the reviewed set prescribing a particular household thermostat number such as “24°C”; Wafier should not manufacture such a number or attribute one to KAPSARC without another verified source.

**ITU-T Y.3172 mapping.** Y.3172 is an ITU telecommunications ML architecture, not Saudi legislation and not a FinTech regulation. It nevertheless gives Wafier a clean, citation-verifiable reference architecture. ITU defines the seven pipeline nodes and the separate MLFO/sandbox components explicitly. citeturn28view3turn29view3turn29view4

| Y.3172 component | ITU function | Wafier mapping |
|---|---|---|
| **SRC — Source** | Source of data supplied to the ML pipeline. citeturn28view3 | Authorized household kWh history, tariff source, weather observations/forecast, calendar features and user budget. Personal-data provenance and consent/basis travel with the source. |
| **C — Collector** | Collects from one or more SRC nodes. citeturn28view3 | Meter/bill import adapter, weather adapter, tariff-source loader and consent-aware ingestion service. |
| **PP — Preprocessor** | Cleans, aggregates or otherwise prepares input for the model. citeturn28view3 | Validate missing/implausible readings; align timestamps/weather; normalize horizons; feature engineering; apply minimization; pseudonymize or anonymize datasets where the applicable analytics design requires it. |

| **P — Policy** | Applies policies to model output and can safeguard application of ML output. citeturn28view3 | **Wafier's deterministic compliance node:** current tariff calculation; confidence threshold; stale-source rejection; purpose/consent gate; no-guarantee wording; RAG citation requirement; escalation/human-review rules. |

| **MLFO** | Manages/orchestrates nodes, model selection and pipeline chaining. citeturn29view3 | Model/version registry, orchestration, source-version management, drift monitoring, audit logs and rollout/rollback management. |
| **ML sandbox** | Isolated environment to train/test/evaluate before live deployment. citeturn29view4 | Backtesting against historical bills/weather, calibration testing, extreme-weather tests, adversarial/RAG citation tests and policy-node regression tests before promotion. |

This is an **engineering adaptation** of Y.3172. ITU's Recommendation is written for ML in future networks including IMT-2020; it does not declare that Saudi consumer applications must implement this architecture. citeturn28view3

**AI forecast liability.** The Saudi Civil Transactions Law supplies a general civil-liability baseline. Article 118 makes its harmful-act chapter applicable, subject to special statutory liability rules, and Article 120 states that a fault causing damage to another requires compensation. citeturn29view0turn29view1 Articles 136–137 state that compensation should repair the damage fully and address losses and lost gain naturally resulting from the harmful act. citeturn29view2 For a PDPL violation, PDPL Article 40 independently provides a right to seek proportionate compensation for material or moral damage. citeturn19view4

SDAIA's AI Ethics document speaks in terms of attributable responsibility and liability, model monitoring and risk mitigation, but it does not create a verified, specialized statutory liability regime saying who pays specifically when an electricity-bill AI forecast differs from the eventual bill. citeturn26view1turn26view2 The correct hackathon characterization is therefore:

> **Policy gap:** no AI-bill-forecast-specific Saudi liability allocation was identified in the reviewed primary sources. General civil, contractual, data-protection and any otherwise applicable sector/consumer law remain relevant according to the facts. The gap is the absence of an AI-forecast-specific rule, not an absence of liability generally.

That statement is deliberately limited to the reviewed official primary-source landscape; research cannot logically prove that no possible Saudi rule anywhere could ever bear on a particular dispute.

## Conflicts & Ambiguities

**“Explicit consent” is stricter than what can safely be claimed as universally mandatory.** PDPL Article 5's consent rule must be read with Article 6's alternative bases and Regulation Article 11's explicit-consent cases. citeturn20view0turn20view2 There is no contradiction in the legal texts; the ambiguity arises when a product requirement shortens them to “PDPL requires explicit consent for energy data.” The safer wording is that Wafier **chooses** explicit opt-in for identifiable energy data while its legal-basis record preserves the PDPL analysis.

**Purpose limitation does not equal a blanket marketing ban.** Articles 10 and 13 restrict incompatible secondary processing, while Article 26 expressly allows certain consented marketing and Regulation Article 29 regulates direct marketing. citeturn19view1turn19view3turn20view4 Hence a Wafier “never target ads using consumption” rule is an excellent privacy control but should not be represented as the literal text of PDPL.

**Pseudonymization does not remove the PDPL relationship.** The Regulation's own definition assumes additional information exists that can reconnect codes and identities, whereas anonymization requires impossible re-identification and then removes the data from Personal Data status. citeturn21view0turn20view2 This is particularly important if the hackathon architecture says “anonymized analytics” while retaining a household-to-token mapping.

**SDAIA AI Ethics has compliance language but is not the same instrument as a statute or the PDPL Implementing Regulation.** The document gives SDAIA roles for monitoring and measuring adoption/compliance and sets detailed ethics practices, but the project should maintain document-type provenance so an ethics principle is not presented to users as a statutory article. citeturn26view3

**The anti-deception principle supports—but does not literally prescribe—a “not guaranteed” disclaimer.** SDAIA explicitly says AI should not deceive people and requires risk mitigation for inaccurate predictions. citeturn26view1turn26view2 The phrase “AI-generated electricity forecasts may not be presented as guaranteed financial outcomes” is therefore a sensible Wafier policy formulation derived from those principles, not a quote from the document.

**SAMA's consumer rules are a strong benchmark with a jurisdictional caveat.** Clear, accurate, non-misleading information and risk warnings fit Wafier's forecast UX extremely well, but SAMA expressly directs those rules at financial institutions. citeturn25view3 An ITU Finance-track label is not a substitute for showing that Wafier is performing a SAMA-regulated financial activity.

**Open Banking can touch an electricity bill without encompassing electricity usage.** A bank-side transaction paying the utility can be Payment Account/Payment Transaction information; the physical meter's kWh time series is not thereby converted into bank-account information. citeturn25view2 Wafier should preserve this provenance distinction because consent, API authorization and regulatory controls differ by source.

**CMA jurisdiction is feature-dependent.** A consumer budgeting tool without securities functionality sits outside the core capital-market mandate shown in CMA's primary sources, but the result can change if investment advice or other securities activity is added. citeturn25view4turn25view6 The relevant question is the regulated activity, not whether the interface contains money values or is described as “FinTech.”

**Saudi Energy's general data-sharing policy should not be over-read.** The existence of a data-sharing request policy and privacy disclosures proves that data sharing occurs under governed conditions; it does not establish that a household can unilaterally delegate real-time smart-meter data to arbitrary apps. citeturn22search1turn24search1 No such direct-app entitlement was verified here.

**Tariff provenance requires care.** The current SERA result supplies the tier threshold and values, but the direct web renderer timed out. citeturn22search0 That is not a basis for inventing anything else—VAT treatment, special charges, billing adjustments, historical effective dates or additional residential calculation rules must come from separately verified primary sources before entering deterministic billing logic.

**Y.3172's “Policy” node is technically useful but not a legal-policy database.** ITU says P applies policies to model output as a safety/control stage. citeturn28view3 Using that node for PDPL purpose gates, tariff logic, anti-deception wording and mandatory-RAG-citation checks is Wafier's architectural mapping. ITU itself does not say those Saudi rules belong there.

The **Civil Transactions Law fallback** is another high-value record. Article 120 supplies a general fault-damage-compensation rule, while Articles 136–137 frame compensable loss. citeturn29view0turn29view2 This allows the submission to articulate the AI-specific liability gap precisely: **Saudi Arabia has general liability law; what was not identified is a bespoke statutory rule allocating responsibility when an AI-generated household electricity forecast is inaccurate.**
